# ANTIGRAVITY SPEC — PLAYER EXPERIENCE, CUTE UX & LEARNING-BY-PLAY

**Version:** 1.0  
**Priority:** VERY HIGH  
**Purpose:** Ensure the final game is not only functionally correct, but also delightful, cute, intuitive, emotionally rewarding, and educational through play.

---

# 1. CORE PRODUCT FEELING

The player should feel all of the following at the same time:

- "Dễ thương"
- "Dễ hiểu"
- "Muốn chạm vào mọi thứ"
- "Chơi vui tay"
- "Không bị quá căng thẳng"
- "Mình đang thật sự điều hành một cửa hàng"
- "Mình đang học được cách vận hành cửa hàng tốt hơn"

The game must NOT feel like:
- a dry admin dashboard
- an ERP system with cute colors
- a spreadsheet with icons
- a hard-core simulation that overwhelms casual players
- a shallow clicker with no business meaning

The target feeling is:

```text
Cozy + Cute + Tactile + Clear + Rewarding + Insightful
```

---

# 2. PLAYER EXPERIENCE PRINCIPLE

Antigravity must optimize for **player feeling**, not only feature count.

Every implemented feature must satisfy at least these 4 dimensions:

1. **Clarity**  
   The player quickly understands what is happening.

2. **Pleasure**  
   The action feels satisfying to perform.

3. **Meaning**  
   The result connects to business logic and progression.

4. **Learning**  
   The player learns something about store operation through cause and effect.

If a feature is correct but feels annoying, confusing or emotionally flat, it is not complete.

---

# 3. CUTENESS IS A SYSTEM, NOT JUST A COLOR CHOICE

“Cute” is not only:
- pastel colors
- rounded icons
- chibi faces

A cute experience also requires:
- soft motion
- warm feedback
- low-friction interaction
- readable layouts
- playful sounds
- friendly microcopy
- charming reactions
- comforting pacing

Antigravity must treat **cuteness as a full UX system**.

---

# 4. INTERACTION DESIGN GOAL

Player actions must feel:
- immediate
- tactile
- smooth
- readable
- rewarding

When a player taps:
- there should be visual feedback
- the result should be understandable
- the game should feel responsive

Every frequent action should produce some combination of:
- soft button press
- object highlight
- bounce
- glow
- tiny particle
- friendly sound
- satisfied character reaction
- clear status update

---

# 5. "FUN TO TOUCH" RULE

Any object that the player frequently interacts with should feel inviting.

Examples:
- racks
- clothing icons
- size chips
- backroom bins
- checkout scanner
- fitting room
- stock boxes
- cleaning tools
- review response choices

Antigravity should ask:
> "Would the player enjoy touching this repeatedly for dozens or hundreds of sessions?"

If not, improve:
- shape
- scale
- feedback
- animation
- spacing
- clarity

---

# 6. CUTE VISUAL LANGUAGE

Preferred visual direction:

- rounded forms
- soft outlines
- warm neutrals
- pastel accent colors
- clean but playful typography
- cozy storefront atmosphere
- cute but not childish to the point of losing clarity
- expressive character reactions
- simple readable product icons/illustrations
- soft panel shadows
- clear visual grouping

Avoid:
- overly realistic retail UI
- sharp corporate styling
- tiny unreadable icons
- aggressive red warning overload
- cluttered dense management screens

---

# 7. EMOTIONAL REWARD LOOP

Every small success should feel good.

Examples:
- correct item delivered
- customer smiles
- fitting success
- payment completed
- review improved
- shelf fully restocked
- stock issue solved
- manager performance improved
- branch profitability restored

Potential feedback:
- smile animation
- heart pop
- sparkle
- money float
- approval sound
- star pulse
- little “Good job!” card
- “Khách rất hài lòng!”
- “Kệ đã đầy hàng!”

But feedback must remain tasteful and not become noisy.

---

# 8. FAILURE SHOULD TEACH, NOT PUNISH HARSHLY

When the player makes a mistake, the game should not only punish them.

It should explain the consequence clearly.

Examples:
- wrong size given
- customer leaves queue
- shelf stockout
- poor shift coverage
- branch profit falls
- reviews drop

The player should think:
> “À, vì mình làm thế nên mới bị thế.”

not:
> “Game này khó chịu và random.”

Design failure as:
- understandable
- recoverable
- educational
- emotionally soft but meaningful

---

# 9. LEARNING THROUGH GAMEPLAY, NOT THROUGH LECTURE

The game must teach business concepts by direct experience.

Examples:

## Inventory lesson
The player learns:
- total stock is not enough if the right size is missing
because:
- customer asks for M
- store has only XL
- sale may be lost

## Staffing lesson
The player learns:
- not enough cashiers increases queue
because:
- they see impatient customers waiting

## Cleanliness lesson
The player learns:
- cleaning affects store quality
because:
- fitting-room complaints appear

## Manager lesson
The player learns:
- a good manager improves the branch
because:
- queue, morale, inventory flow and reviews improve

## Cash flow lesson
The player learns:
- buying too much inventory hurts cash
because:
- they run low on available money even when stock is high

Do NOT overuse long educational text blocks.

---

# 10. MICRO-LEARNING FORMAT

Educational information should appear in compact, friendly forms:

- short tooltips
- “Why?” buttons
- end-of-day insights
- advisor summaries
- consequence cards
- small coaching notes
- side-by-side comparisons
- visual KPI change callouts

Example:

```text
Hôm nay khách ít hơn hôm qua

Vì sao?
- Review gần đây giảm
- 2 sản phẩm bán chạy bị hết size M
- Thời gian chờ thanh toán tăng
```

This is far better than a long tutorial paragraph.

---

# 11. UI MUST REDUCE COGNITIVE LOAD

The player should not feel mentally overloaded.

Antigravity must:
- show only what matters most right now
- keep each screen focused
- group related information
- reveal detail progressively
- avoid showing every KPI everywhere
- prioritize current task, urgency, and direct next action

Examples:

Good:
- one clear current step
- one clear current customer request
- one clear “problem to solve”

Bad:
- 18 metrics, 7 popups, 5 warnings and 4 blinking buttons at once

---

# 12. FRIENDLY GAME LANGUAGE

Microcopy must be warm, clear and easy.

Prefer:
- “Chọn size khách cần”
- “Kệ này gần hết hàng”
- “Khách đang chờ thanh toán”
- “Chi nhánh này cần thêm nhân viên giờ cao điểm”
- “Khách hài lòng hơn rồi”

Avoid:
- robotic technical text
- enterprise jargon
- long system messages

The player should feel guided, not processed.

---

# 13. CHARACTER EXPRESSIVENESS

Characters are important to emotional feel.

Customer/avatar/employee expressions should communicate:
- happy
- neutral
- waiting
- excited
- frustrated
- disappointed
- satisfied
- grateful

Do this with:
- facial expression
- small motion
- icon bubble
- reaction cue

A cute, expressive reaction often teaches more quickly than a text paragraph.

---

# 14. AUDIO MOOD

Sound design must support:
- coziness
- satisfaction
- lightness
- clarity

Examples:
- soft tap
- cute pickup sound
- successful fitting cue
- cash register cue
- positive review sound
- gentle warning ping
- store opening ambience
- subtle crowd/store ambience

Avoid:
- harsh alarms
- overly loud industrial sounds
- stressful noise spam

Allow:
- BGM volume
- SFX volume
- mute

---

# 15. ANIMATION MOOD

Animations should feel:
- soft
- cheerful
- readable
- not too fast
- not too slow

Preferred animation language:
- bounce
- ease-out
- soft scale
- slide
- tiny sparkles
- floating reward
- pulse for highlighted objects

Avoid:
- aggressive shaking
- chaotic flashing
- too many simultaneous particles
- unclear transitions

---

# 16. TOUCH-FIRST ERGONOMICS

Because the game is portrait and mobile-first:

- tap targets must be comfortable
- important actions must not be tiny
- repeated actions should be easy on fingers
- the main work area must not feel cramped
- feedback must happen at the touch location when appropriate

The game should feel “pleasant to operate” even after many minutes of continuous play.

---

# 17. SATISFYING WORKFLOW DESIGN

A workflow should feel like a small accomplishment.

Example customer service flow:
1. understand request
2. pick correct item
3. prepare or deliver
4. customer likes it
5. checkout succeeds
6. positive reaction appears

This structure creates a complete emotional mini-loop.

Every core workflow should have:
- clear start
- satisfying middle
- rewarding resolution

---

# 18. MAKE WORK FEEL PLAYFUL

The game is about work, but should not feel like labor in a negative sense.

Tasks like:
- restocking
- receiving boxes
- counting stock
- cleaning
- processing returns
- scheduling staff

must be designed so they are:
- understandable
- light
- tactile
- satisfying
- not overlong
- not repetitive without variation

The player should feel:
> “This is fun store work.”

not:
> “This is admin homework.”

---

# 19. PROGRESS SHOULD FEEL EMOTIONALLY RICH

Player progression should not be only numerical.

Progress should feel visible and emotional:

- store looks nicer
- shelves look fuller
- customers react more positively
- more varied products appear
- employee team feels more alive
- branch map grows
- visual identity matures
- the store becomes more charming

The player should feel proud of the business they built.

---

# 20. THE GAME MUST REWARD CARE

If the player carefully manages:
- stock
- customer service
- scheduling
- cleanliness
- reviews
- fitting support
- layout
- promotions

then the player should see meaningful improvement.

This creates:
- mastery
- learning
- attachment
- replay motivation

---

# 21. INFORMATION TONE

When the game presents data, it must still feel game-like.

Instead of:
- dry table only
- too much raw accounting language

prefer:
- illustrated KPI cards
- mini summaries
- visual comparison
- advisor explanations
- trend arrows
- simple business language first
- deeper details on demand

Example:

```text
Doanh thu tăng
nhưng lợi nhuận giảm

Vì sao?
- Giảm giá nhiều hơn
- Chi phí nhân sự tăng
- Tỉ lệ đổi trả cao hơn
```

---

# 22. MOMENTS OF DELIGHT

Antigravity should intentionally create small delightful moments:

- first successful sale of the day
- perfect service
- five-star review
- employee level-up
- fully restocked wall
- sold-out best seller
- first branch opening
- first profitable week
- customer compliments store style
- fitting-room save (bringing the right size in time)
- recovery of a struggling branch

These moments are critical to emotional retention.

---

# 23. MOMENTS OF STRESS MUST BE MANAGEABLE

Some stress is good:
- queue growing
- low stock
- impatience
- branch issue
- event/problem

But stress should be:
- readable
- solvable
- not constant
- not overwhelming every minute

The player should feel:
> “Mình phải xử lý việc này.”

not:
> “Mình bị công việc đè chết.”

---

# 24. "WHY" FEEDBACK IS MANDATORY

Whenever possible, the game should explain why something changed.

Examples:
- “Khách giảm vì review thấp gần đây”
- “Chi nhánh này lỗ vì thuê mặt bằng cao và doanh thu thấp”
- “Nhân viên mệt vì tăng ca liên tục”
- “Tỉ lệ đổi trả cao vì size không phù hợp”
- “Fitting room giúp tăng tỉ lệ mua thành công”

This turns the game into a learning experience.

---

# 25. PLAYER SHOULD FEEL SMARTER OVER TIME

The game should make the player feel:

```text
"Lúc đầu mình làm theo hướng dẫn.
Bây giờ mình hiểu cách cửa hàng vận hành."
```

This means:
- tutorials guide initially
- later the player starts predicting outcomes
- game systems become intuitively understandable
- player decisions become more confident

This is one of the most important educational goals.

---

# 26. PROGRESSIVE COMPLEXITY

Do not dump every system on Day 1.

Introduce complexity in layers:

## Early
- basic fulfillment
- fitting
- checkout
- shelf restock

## Mid
- staff
- schedule
- reviews
- returns
- promotions

## Late
- branch management
- transfer
- analytics
- warehouse
- crisis handling

Cute and approachable UX becomes much more important as complexity grows.

---

# 27. BUSINESS DEPTH MUST BE HIDDEN INSIDE SIMPLE INTERACTIONS

This is a key product principle.

The player should feel:
- the interface is simple
- the actions are easy to do
- the consequences are meaningful

That means:
- low interaction friction
- high business consequence

Example:
Choosing a shift is simple,
but the result affects:
- payroll
- queue
- morale
- reviews
- conversion

This is ideal design.

---

# 28. UI DESIGN ACCEPTANCE QUESTIONS

For every screen or interaction, Antigravity must ask:

1. Is this cute enough?
2. Is this easy to understand at a glance?
3. Is this satisfying to touch?
4. Does the player understand the next action?
5. Does the feedback feel emotionally rewarding?
6. Is the business effect understandable?
7. Does it teach something useful?
8. Is it calm enough visually?
9. Is it still game-like rather than enterprise-like?
10. Would a player enjoy repeating this action many times?

If the answer is weak, redesign.

---

# 29. PLAYER FEELING KPIs (DESIGN CHECKLIST)

These are not hard coded game KPIs, but design goals Antigravity should self-evaluate:

- Time to understand current objective
- Number of taps for frequent tasks
- Readability of important info
- Emotional reward density
- Friction during repeated actions
- Perceived cuteness/coziness
- Clarity of cause-and-effect
- Screen overwhelm level
- Delight moments per session
- Learning moments per session

---

# 30. EXAMPLES OF GOOD EXECUTION

## Good item fulfillment
The player sees:
- cute customer request
- clear size/color info
- highlighted rack
- nice tap feedback
- item appears on prep table
- customer smiles when correct
- later payment creates satisfying reward
- end-of-day note explains that fast service helped reviews

This is both fun and educational.

## Good staffing lesson
The player sees:
- queue growing
- one cashier overloaded
- subtle warning
- easy staff reassignment
- queue improves
- end-of-day insight:
  "Tăng 1 thu ngân giờ cao điểm giúp giảm thời gian chờ 28%"

This teaches through play.

---

# 31. EXAMPLES OF BAD EXECUTION

Bad examples:
- long blocks of text explaining business concepts
- tiny buttons in a crowded screen
- money increasing before payment
- unclear penalties without explanation
- too many warnings at once
- visual clutter
- all business analysis shown as raw tables only
- cute art but stressful/annoying interaction
- deep systems hidden behind confusing menus
- educational content disconnected from what the player just did

---

# 32. CUTE UX + LEARNING IN EACH SYSTEM

Antigravity must apply both delight and learning to every major system:

## Fulfillment
Cute:
- charming request bubble
- satisfying item placement
Learn:
- exact size/color matters

## Fitting room
Cute:
- expressive reactions
Learn:
- support and speed affect conversion

## Checkout
Cute:
- tidy item scan feedback
Learn:
- sale only happens at payment

## Restock
Cute:
- shelves visibly refill
Learn:
- availability matters

## Staff scheduling
Cute:
- drag-and-drop or friendly scheduling UI
Learn:
- right staffing reduces queue and stress

## Reviews
Cute:
- readable, friendly review cards
Learn:
- service quality affects future demand

## Branch management
Cute:
- charming branch cards/map markers
Learn:
- revenue ≠ profit, market fit matters

---

# 33. TONE OF THE FINAL GAME

The final game should feel like:

```text
A delightful cute fashion shop game
that secretly teaches strong store-operation thinking.
```

Not:

```text
A training software disguised as a game.
```

When the player finishes a session, they should feel:

- "Game này dễ thương thật"
- "Chơi rất vui tay"
- "Mình hiểu cửa hàng vận hành tốt hơn"
